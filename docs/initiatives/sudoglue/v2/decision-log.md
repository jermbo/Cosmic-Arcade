# Sudoglue v2 decisions

## D1. The look is soft clay

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** Which look should Sudoglue have?

**Options:** A prototype on the real board showed six styles: A frosted glass with one dock, B paper and pastel, C night glow with a ring menu, D a 5×5 pair palette, E quiet mono with side columns, F soft clay with one flipping row.

**Choice:** F, the soft clay look.

**Reason:** The owner liked F the most. The neo-brutalist look of 1.0 did not fit a calm daily puzzle. The owner also liked the side columns of E, which led to round 2.

## D2. The layout is the thumb zone

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** Which layout should the play screen lock in?

**Options:** A second prototype showed four layouts in clay, light and dark: A a flipping row, B side columns, C thumb zone (tools on top, the number and color rows at the bottom), D a pop-up picker at the tile.

**Choice:** C, the thumb zone.

**Reason:** It gives a big board with every control in reach of the thumb. Numbers and colors are both always in view, and it works the same way with a keyboard and a gamepad.

## D3. A shop with coins

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** How do players get new looks?

**Options:** Unlock by playing; all free in Settings; a shop with coins.

**Choice:** A shop with coins, like Wordkin. It sells themes, tile styles and sound packs.

**Reason:** The arcade's other games use a shop. Three kinds of item give the shop variety.

## D4. Coins per window, with bonuses; hints are free

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** How do players earn coins?

**Options:** Per window with bonuses; per window only; per window with hints that cost coins.

**Choice:** A base by level, plus bonuses for no hints, a new best time and the daily streak. Hints never cost coins.

**Reason:** It rewards clean play, but a player who asks for help to learn the logic is never punished.

## D5. A starter set in the shop

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** How big is the shop at launch?

**Options:** Starter set (6 themes, 4 tile styles, 3 sound packs); small; large.

**Choice:** The starter set. The first item of each kind is free, and the first dark theme is free too, so dark mode is there from the start.

**Reason:** Enough to want things, without thin filler. The free dark theme is an accessibility choice.

## D6. Build what is decided, settle details in the game

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** Prototype the themes and tile styles first, or build now?

**Choice:** Build the restyle, the layout, the shop and the coins now, and tune the exact themes and sounds in the real game.

**Reason:** The owner chose to build. All shop items are data, so they are easy to change after a playtest.

## D7. No effect for a finished row or column

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** Should a full row or column get a success effect?

**Choice:** No. Remove the hop, the light band, the glints, the chime and the spoken "row done". Only the solved window is celebrated.

**Reason:** A full row with no clash is not known to be right, so the effect gave a false "correct". The hop also moved the whole tile and fought its lift, so tiles snapped in and out of place and broke the player's flow.

## D8. Tuned colors and optional shapes, not patterns

**Date:** 2026-11-27

**Question:** The patterns on the colors did not work for the owner. How else can color stay accessible?

**Options:** Shapes as a setting; shapes always on; letters (R, C, A, J); colors alone, tuned for color blindness.

**Choice:** Two of them together. The patterns go. Every theme's colors are tuned to stay apart for color-blind players, and a **Color shapes** setting (● ruby, ▲ cobalt, ■ amber, ◆ jade) adds a symbol for players who need more. A test checks the colors on every run.

**Reason:** The board stays clean for most players. The tuning helps everyone with no setting at all. The shapes are clear and calm, and letters would sit too close to the numbers. A simulation showed ruby and jade were almost the same for deuteranopia before the tuning.
