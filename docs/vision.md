# Vision

I've been a developer for a long time, and for most of that time there's been a list of games in the back of my head that I always meant to make someday. A tower defense. A roguelike deck builder. A cozy little city builder. A JRPG, because of course. A couple of tabletop RPGs I keep scribbling notes for.

This arcade is where someday finally happens.

## Why now

I use AI every day at work, and honestly, I'm still figuring it out. Work isn't a great place to experiment, though. There's always a deadline and always something on the line. Games felt like the right sandbox. Every genre pulls on a different muscle, every engine makes me think a little differently, and nobody gets hurt if I break something.

So really this is three things at once: a place to finally build these games, a place to learn what AI is actually good at (and where it falls flat), and a place to have fun. If a game stops being fun to make, I'll set it down and pick up something else.

## Who it's for

Mostly me, if I'm being honest. But I'm building it in public on purpose. If you're a developer curious how someone actually uses AI day to day, the messy parts included, I hope this is useful. And if you've always wanted to make a game and never started, maybe this gives you a little push.

## Being open about AI

I want to be upfront about this. There's an AI disclosure you can get to from anywhere on the site. And every game page has the real story of how it got built: where AI helped, where it didn't, and what I ended up doing myself.

## What to expect

Most games here will be playable, something you can sit down with for a few minutes and enjoy. Some will turn into more. Some will stay rough. That's fine. If I learned something along the way, it counts.

This isn't a business. There's nothing for sale, no ads, no accounts. It's not a tutorial site, and it's not taking submissions. It's just my stuff. There's no backend right now either. If a game ever really needs a leaderboard, I'll cross that bridge then.

And it should feel like walking into an old arcade. Neon, cabinets, a bit of noise. Every game is a machine you walk up to and play.

## A year from now

I'd love to look back and see a handful of games I'm proud of, each built a different way. I'd love to have a real feel for where AI pulls its weight. Mostly, I want to still be enjoying it.

And if somebody tells me this got them to start their own game? That would make my year.
